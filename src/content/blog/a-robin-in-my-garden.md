I've ventured into gardening, one of those love-hate relationships that seem to come with owning a house. Some days I enjoy working outside, with the sunshine on my face and fresh air in my lungs for once. Other days I question whether it's a good use of my weekends, when I could be doing nothing instead. Nevertheless, it's one of those things I have to do to keep the garden from invading the house.

I've kept most of the garden as it was before I arrived, with a few additions of my own. I planted some berry bushes, hoping they will reward me with fruit next year. Between the labour and the CAPEX, this endeavour is hardly justified from an economic point of view, even with the outrageous price of berries these days.

As I water my plants and trim unruly branches, I hesitate before each cut like any rookie gardener, as though the bushes were the most delicate creatures on Earth. As I work, I begin to notice the uninvited creatures that visit the garden. Squirrels and cats use the garden as a passage to other lands, sneaky foxes occasionally seek a place to sleep, and birds come and go among the bushes and trees.

Of all these selfish visitors, two robins caught my eye. Every so often, they would pop their heads out from between the bushes. I took a liking to them, so I installed a bird feeder. Every couple of days, I would spot them enjoying the unexpected food. It was a nice spectacle, watching them come and go. For a few moments, they offered a moment of calmness to my usual stream of thoughts and ruminations.

Encouraged by their visit. I decided to install a bird house. The location wasn't ideal, close to the storage garden where passing cats could reach it. Perhaps that's why it has remained empty ever since. I haven't lost hope, though. The bird bath has met a similar fate. I've only seen algae making use of it.

As the days went by, I noticed that the bird feeder was emptying at an alarming rate. The size of the robins hardly justified the amount of food that was disappearing. A full feeder would be down to a third the next morning and completely empty by the second day. I had my suspicions but never any hard proof that squirrels, pigeons or even me sleepwalking were responsible. I kept filling it anyway, knowing that at least once in a while some food would reach the robins. 

Spring arrives. Working hours increase in the garden. Around this time, I began noticing a single robing appearing whenever I was outside. It would show up from the hedges, perch on nearby branches, or fly across the garden to settle on top of an outdoor light. At first, it kept a safe distance, observing my movements before darting from one end of the garden to the other.

I decided to believe that this robin was one of the two that had previously visited my bird feeder. I preferred not to think too much about what happened to the other. Perhaps, a falling out, a critical illness, or an amicable separation. Whatever the reason, I was happy to see that at least one of them was still around.

As the weeks went by, the robin began getting closer. It would appear while I pulled weeds, watered plants or prepared the soil, perching on nearby branches or hopping along the ground just beyond my reach. Sometimes it would pause, tilt its head and look at me from one side. I would look back. Those black eyes of pure curiosity. It allowed me to look at it.

It is a beautiful bird. Its orange chest and round belly give it a rather dignified appearance. Like an obese king. I think it should ease up on the berries and worms. It hops around with its tiny legs, occasionally stopping to let out a few melodic notes before returning to its business. Sometimes, it would hop over to inspect my gardening work, like a supervisor deciding whether my work was up to its standards.

Autumn arrives. The weather turns colder, and I spend less time in the garden. I haven't seen the robin for a while. Perhaps it stills visits, perching on the bushes looking through the window while I am inside. I like to think so.

Whenever you are, please know that I appreciate you. You made my days a little better.

I am grateful for your uninvited visit.
