---
title: 'I Like Coffee Shops'
description: 'On the pleasures of coffee shops'
pubDate: 'Oct 17 2026'
---


One of my pleasures in life is going to coffee shops. Coffee in hand, enjoying a good read, or simply thinking and watching people go about their day outside.

I like coffee shops. Spaces connecting strangers through noise and warmth. People sit in close proximity, not exchanging a single word, only the occasional cheeky glance. Yet, the atmosphere makes you feel like you belong. You feel at ease and can comfortably enjoy a cup of coffee.

I order lattes or espressos most of the time. Unfortunately, my palate is not sophisticated enough to savour the differences between beans and blends. Maybe my barbaric genes are still too dominant.

Other times, when I feel adventurous and want to shake things up, I order a flat white or a Spanish latte. This kind of covers most of the available options in the UK. I was ecstatic when I found a place serving London Fog (not even coffee, but still).

A similar phenomenon occurs with pastries. Carrot cakes, croissants, muffins, and brownies are staples in most coffee shops, with a few variations here and there.

My free-thinking hat tells me there has been a series of referendums I was unaware of (or perhaps I wasn't invited to) in which society collectively decided what coffees and pastries it likes. Customers demand, and coffee shops oblige.

Such is life in the UK.

My journey starts with deciding which coffee shop to visit. I avoid small coffee shops where I might be singled out by customers waiting for a table or by staff eager to take my money and see me leave. I target places where I can blend in, one among many, allowing me to stay longer than it takes me to finish my drink.

Once I find a place and order a coffee, I select a nice table with a good view and cement myself to the chair for hours.

I take great pleasure in knowing that I will be taking a great deal of overhead costs for the price of a latte. The coffee shop would throw me out in a heartbeat if they could.

I look out the window and smile with satisfaction at my free-riding, knowing that I make things worse for the coffee shop, other consumers and society as a whole.

Sometimes things are not as easy or enjoyable. The number of people in the coffee shop is a critical parameter to keep tabs on. Too few and too dispersed, and I feel the pressure of judgemental eyes on me. *A single coffee and you've been here for three hours? You are breaking the social contract! How inconsiderate and shameless you are!*

I get increasingly nervous. I look around for those accusatory eyes, but whenever I meet someone's gaze, I immediately avert my own, too afraid to confront them.

Gravity increases. The air becomes suffocating.

*I've been found out.*

More people arrive. Tables fill up. The noise increases and the pressure eases.

Of course, my peace does not last long as the number of customer reaches critical mass. I notice new arrivals scouring the room for available seats. My heart beats harder, and the pressure builds around my temples. My brain shouts: *I am exposed!*

A person approaches my table and asks for a chair. I freeze like a deer caught in headlights. *Yes, of course,* I whisper as they take the chair and effortlessly separate it from my table.

A shiver runs down my spine. I feel the sharp pain of a limb being ripped from my body. Blood pours profusely for everyone to see.

As if a single amputation weren't punishment enough, God sometimes condemns his noblest warriors to endure the same ordeal again. And again. By the end of this relentless onslaught, I am reduced to a limbless torso, clinging to the last chair in my possession. *Despicable!* These strangers are probably chuckling to themselves as my mutilated body is left exposed to the elements. No action possible. Just howling.

Sometimes, at the pinnacle of insolence, someone comes to my table and asks if they can sit with me.

*Who the fuck do they think they are?*

I spew bile within the walls of my mind at this antisocial and brazen act. *Do they go through life thinking they can inconvenience others with impunity? Would they be happy if I knocked on their door, walked into their house, and asked to sit at their dining table?*

I look down on such creatures who resort to such disgusting behaviour. But, as a noble being, compassionate and understanding towards lesser beings, I refuse to let their insolence disturb me. I stay another ten minutes, long enough to establish that I am not intimidated, before leaving my table.

I am not surrendering. I am acting with grace and restraint. They might feel victorious, but the dignity stays with me.

Even if none of this happens, there is a final act of humiliation reserved for us free-riders. The moment they take your mug away and you can no longer pretend to drink the dust at the bottom of your cup; hours after finishing your coffee.

The spell is broken. A signal from the higher-ups that your time has come.

I leave for now.

But I will be back. I like coffee shops.
