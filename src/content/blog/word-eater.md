Some of our identities remain in the fantasy world [^remain-hypothetical]. They give us the carrot and the stick. Others invite us to walk towards them.

Perhaps it's a motivational video, a book, or a fleeting moment of self-reflection between mindless tasks, daily worries and sleep.

Whatever that trigger, there's a sudden rush of optimism. Your mind takes the fantasy into overdrive while frantically devising a plan. And here's the trap: the plans are usually simple, but never straightforward.

*Do you want to play guitar and write music?* your brain enthusiastically asks. *First, you need to research the best guitars for beginners, but for that, you need to understand what makes a good guitar. And don't forget the strings. Maybe reading a book about this topic would be good idea. Maybe a forum thread with thousands of comments fiercely discussing wood types and the thickness and material of the strings would help you make an informed decision.*

And don't forget about the lessons, which lead to further research into whether it's best to pay for a teacher or do it solo. 

And don't forget about the best tips to speed up your progress.

And don't forget yourself.

How can you possibly learn all of this if not by studying? Of course, taking the first step towards your ideal without proper preparation is unwise and ungentlemanly. You might make a misstep. Even worse, a step in the wrong direction! The horror for the efficiency junkie. Why walk like a drunkard when you can prepare first and then run sober? Why waste your time doing things the wrong way?

Off you go, then, with almost childish optimism. To the videos, blogs, discussion forums, books... but not just any books... the best books, of course.

*First*, you say, * I am going to get a general idea of what I need to know* (sure, buddy!). *Then I'm going to practice, following the guidance of the best resources*. 

And you read, read and read.

The content gives you a rush. You feel like you are closing the distance between your current self and your fantasy. You don't question what you are reading. How could you? you barely know anything to begin with. 

You keep reading. Retain 10% at most. THat drops to 2% in a few days. 

Doesn't matter. There's always another book, another video, another expert who has figured out everything that needs figuring out.

No thoughts, no pause for reflection. Just consumption. Eating words. 

Several days passes and you feel good after each one, proud of your hard work. You might open a cold one in celebration.

Your gobble of words might pay off. You buy a guitar. It becomes a bit dusty as you gobble more words.

You get tired of reading. You decide to put learning guitar on hold for a bit.

You pick the guitar, and it's your moment to shine. Nothing comes out of it. Of course, you have no coordination, and your hands are as weak as they were before the thoughts of learning guitar even crossed your mind.

You practice your scales. After all, studies have shown that it's the most efficient way to build a strong foundation and proper technique.

Boring. Better watch Youtube. 

Let's just leave the guitar in the corner, collecting dust.
